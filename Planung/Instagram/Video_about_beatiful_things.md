# Instagram Reel #5 — Script (English)

Status: **Draft, ready for review**
Length: ~45-50 seconds (~125 words)
Format: Reel, vertical, talking-head + motion graphics / B-roll
Standalone topic — not a build-in-public episode, but it ends on one detail from the app
as proof that you do this yourself. Source text: the LinkedIn-style post above.

Core idea in one line: **AI made "good enough" free — so the only thing left worth paying
for is the stuff someone actually cared about.** Steve Jobs and the inside of the Mac is the
picture that makes it stick.

---

## Hook (0:00–0:06)

**Shot:** You, straight to camera, close. No intro, no "hey guys".

**Voiceover:**
> "Steve Jobs obsessed over a part of the computer that no customer would ever see."

**On-screen text:**
> THE PART NOBODY SEES

**Animation:** The text is typed in letter by letter, then "NOBODY" gets struck through
with a quick hand-drawn line and stays on screen for a beat.

**Transition out:** Hard cut on the last word — no fade. The hook must not feel soft.

---

## The Story (0:06–0:20)

**Shot:** B-roll instead of your face: a closed device (laptop, old Mac, phone) lying on a
table, then it opens / the back comes off and the circuit board is visible. If you can't
film that: a slow push-in on a photo of a circuit board.

**Voiceover:**
> "Apple products, espacially those in Jobs eara aren't made to be opened. But if you opened one anyway, the inside
> looked as clean as the outside. Jobs knew almost nobody would ever look in there. He
> built it beautifully anyway."

**On-screen text (one line at a time, each replacing the last):**
> NOT MADE TO BE OPENED
> BEAUTIFUL ANYWAY

**Animation:** "Exploded view" — the device separates into layers that float apart (case,
board, battery), each layer sliding up a bit further than the one below. Cheap version: a
mask wipe that reveals the inside from the outside, like an X-ray sliding across.

**Transition in:** Match cut — your hand closing in the hook → a hand on the device.
**Transition out:** Fast zoom *into* the circuit board until it blurs, landing on the next
shot.

---

## The Turn (0:20–0:33)

**Shot:** Screen recording: a prompt goes in, a generic landing page / app comes out in
seconds. Then the same result tiled 6-9 times across the screen — all looking identical.

**Voiceover:**
> "Today, AI builds you something mediocre in one prompt. Which means everybody can. And
> if everybody can make it, nobody will pay for it."

**On-screen text:**
> ONE PROMPT = AVERAGE
> EVERYONE HAS AVERAGE

**Animation:** The single screen duplicates into a grid (1 → 4 → 9 → 16), each copy
popping in on the beat, until the tiles are too small to read. Then the whole grid drains
to grey.

**Transition in:** The zoom from the circuit board resolves into the screen recording
(zoom-through).
**Transition out:** One tile in the grid stays in colour and scales up to fill the frame —
that's you / your project.

---

## The Proof (0:33–0:42)

**Shot:** Screen recording of your app's zoom: tap 1× → 5×, slowed down so the smooth
ramp is visible. Then you, straight to camera.

**Voiceover:**
> "In my camera app, I spent hours on how the zoom *feels*. Nobody will ever notice. That's
> the point."

**On-screen text:**
> NOBODY WILL NOTICE.
> THAT'S THE POINT.

**Animation:** Split screen, left "linear" and right "what I shipped" — two dots moving
along a line, the left one crawling then jumping, the right one gliding. Two seconds, no
explanation needed.

**Transition out:** Simple cut back to your face. After three animated sections, the calm
is the effect.

---

## CTA (0:42–0:48)

**Shot:** You, straight to camera, slower than the rest of the video.

**Voiceover:**
> "So build the part nobody sees. What's something you made better than it had to be?"

**On-screen text:**
> BUILD BEAUTIFUL THINGS.

**Animation:** Text fades in slowly, no bounce, no pop — the one moment in the video that
takes its time. Hold for a full second after you stop talking.

---

## Animation & Transition Overview

| Section | Animation | Transition out | Effort |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Typed text, strike-through on "NOBODY" | Hard cut | low |
| Story | Exploded view / X-ray wipe | Zoom into the board | **high** (X-ray wipe: medium) |
| Turn | Screen multiplies into a grid, drains to grey | One tile scales up | medium |
| Proof | Two dots: linear vs. smooth | Plain cut | low |
| CTA | Slow fade, long hold | — | low |

Three rules that hold it together:

1. **One visual idea per section.** Each animation shows exactly what the sentence says —
   opening up, multiplying, gliding. Nothing decorative.
2. **The transitions tell the story too:** *into* the device (looking inside), *out* to the
   grid (the mass), *back in* on one tile (the one that's different).
3. **The video has to pass its own test.** A reel about craftsmanship with stock transitions
   and a default caption font contradicts itself. Rather two animations done properly than
   five templates. If time is short: keep the grid and the two dots, drop the exploded view.

Sound: a soft click when the device opens, a quick rising tick for each grid step, then
silence under the last line.

## Production Notes

- **Language:** the original post says "templated shit" and "did not give a shit". The
  voiceover avoids swearing — Instagram tends to limit reach for it in the first seconds,
  and the point lands without it. If you want the edge back, put it in the caption, not
  the hook.
- **Don't use Apple footage or product shots from the internet** — film your own device or
  use a board you can photograph yourself. Same for a photo of Jobs: skip it, his name is
  enough.
- The exploded view is the most expensive shot. An honest alternative that fits the theme
  even better: actually unscrew an old device on camera, in time-lapse.
- At ~48 s it's the longest reel so far. First thing to cut if it drags: the second line of
  The Turn ("Which means everybody can.").

## Honesty Check (what's verified vs. not)

- **The Jobs story is real, but be precise about which product.** The well-documented case
  is the original Macintosh (1984): Jobs insisted the circuit board look neat although the
  case was sealed, and had the team's signatures put inside the case. He traced it back to
  his father, who taught him to build the back of a cabinet as well as the front. "All
  Apple products" (as in the post) is a stretch — the script says "Apple products aren't
  made to be opened", which is fair, and keeps the beauty claim tied to Jobs.
- **"He didn't care about profit" is your reading, not a fact** — Apple became one of the
  most profitable companies ever. The script therefore leaves that sentence out and only
  says what he did. If you want it in, phrase it as "he didn't do it for the money".
- **Which Senra episode?** The post doesn't say. Name it in the caption (Founders has
  several Jobs episodes) — people will ask.
- **The zoom detail is true:** tapping a zoom level animates over 300 ms and interpolates
  geometrically instead of linearly. "Hours" — only say that if it was. And it has so far
  only been seen in the emulator, where the camera image itself stutters; record the shot
  on the real phone and check it actually looks smooth before building a beat on it.
