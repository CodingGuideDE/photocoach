## Caption (for the post)

You've probably used ChatGPT or Claude — but do you actually know what's happening under the hood? I spent a lot of time digging into this, and it genuinely changed how I use these tools.

The core trick: it predicts the next word, based on your prompt and whatever context/memory it has — trained on a massive amount of text until that prediction got really, really good.

Turns out if you get good enough at that, you implicitly pick up grammar, facts, reasoning, even code — because it's all baked into the billions of parameters it adjusted while learning.

It's also exactly why they can confidently say wrong things: predicting what sounds likely isn't the same as checking what's true.

## Full Script (for editing)

You've probably used ChatGPT or Claude but don't really know how it works under the hood. I spent long hours deeply understanding LLMs and it really helps me use them optimally.

What these LLMs are really doing is predicting the next word based on the context it is given by your promt and the memory it has stored about you.

 It learned how to predict the next word by reading an insane amount of text, basically a huge chunk of the internet — and adjusting billions of internal numbers, called parameters until its guesses got really, really good.

Turns out, if you get good enough at predicting the next word, you have to implicitly learn grammar, facts, reasoning patterns, even how to write code because all of that shows up in the text it was trained on. 

That's the whole trick, at the core: predict the next word, at a scale big enough that it starts looking like thinking.
